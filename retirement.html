Optimal Early-Retirement & Happiness Calculator

Money buys different amounts of happiness at different ages, and free time is worth more when you're young and healthy than when you're frail. This tool models both, projects your wealth with compounding, and finds the semi-retirement / FIRE age that maximizes your lifetime happiness — not just your net worth.

All figures in today's dollars; use a real (after-inflation) return. Educational model, not financial advice.

How this works & the research behind it

Wealth. Each year your portfolio compounds at your expected return. While working you add savings (income − spending); once retired you withdraw your spending from the portfolio. If it can no longer cover your spending, consumption is capped at whatever's left.

Happiness. Each year of life earns happiness from two sources:

  • Money — moneyEmphasis × enjoymentAbility(age) × ln(1 + spending/satisfies). The ln captures diminishing marginal utility: happiness rises with the logarithm of income, so the first dollars matter far more than the last.
  • Freedom — freedomEmphasis × freedomValue(age) × freeTimeFraction. Retired = a fully free year; working = only the slice of free time you set.

The enjoyment-ability and freedom-value curves both rise out of childhood (kids face real limits) and fade with declining health — the single biggest multiplier on what you can do with money and time. The tool then tries every retirement age and picks the one with the highest total. Retire too early and you starve the high-value middle years of money; retire too late and you burn your healthiest, freest years at work.

Grounded in:

  • Killingsworth, Kahneman & Mellers (2023), Income and emotional well-being: A conflict resolved — well-being rises with log income. PNAS
  • Bill Perkins, Die With Zero — the utility of money declines with age; fulfillment = f(health, money, free time), with health the largest factor. Interview
  • Blanchett, Estimating the True Cost of Retirement — the retirement "spending smile" (go-go / slow-go / no-go years). Kitces
  • Blanchflower — the U-shaped happiness curve over the lifespan, lowest near age 47. PMC
  • Healthspan vs. lifespan: disability-free life expectancy ≈ 63–64 years. Eurostat
This is a simplified educational model in real (inflation-adjusted) dollars. It ignores taxes, market volatility/sequence-of-returns risk, healthcare shocks, Social Security/pensions, and the genuine uncertainty of your own lifespan. Treat the "optimal age" as a thinking tool, not advice.
